## 1. The two laws

- **Narrative > Numbers.** "Data may be more truthful, but in the battle between narrative and numbers, most of the time humanity picks narrative." — *Storytelling*
- **Emotion > Insight.** "Making people feel something bests any business insight." — *Storytelling*
- "Money, mates, and meaning are all moths to the flame of storytelling." — *Storytelling*

## 2. Core moves (see SKILL.md for the full treatment)

- **The Wow Test** — "If a data point, sentence, or slide doesn't challenge or surprise you, cut it." Seek data with "outsized qualitative impact," not bland stats.
- **Data Elevation** — never state a big number raw; translate to time / size / object / baseline.
- **The Hook** — first line's only job is to make the second unskippable.
- **Assume Intimacy** — "I assume the listener knows a lot and likes me… We're friends."
- **More Drama** — his literal editing note to his team (per analyst Mia Silverio). Drama is added in revision, on purpose.
- **Zoom Out** — from "what happened" to "so what / what made it possible / what happens next."

## 3. Extended moves

### The Crocs Test
Puncture hype by comparing it to something absurdly mundane. When "metaverse" searches peaked, Galloway noted people Googled Crocs more than "the supposed future of human interaction." Deflation by comparison. He used this same Google-Trends-vs-random-benchmark method to call the collapses of Quibi, NFTs, and Virgin Galactic. **Use when:** a draft is buying into hype uncritically and needs a skeptical, funny gut-check.

### Anchored Provocation
"Young people need to drink more." "The era of the brand is over." Provocative openers — but the rule is absolute: **"provocative statements only work when they're anchored in evidence."** Provocation without proof is just noise. **Use when:** you want a contrarian hook; always immediately back it with the data or logic that makes it true.

### Vulnerability
"A good way to connect with several hundred strangers at once… is to be vulnerable." The bridge to an audience is built from the things most people hide. **Use when:** a piece is all competence and no humanity — a confessed failure, fear, or flaw earns more trust than another win.

### Visualization
"Humans process images 60,000× faster than text." Pair data with a visual and always give the baseline alongside it (aviation's 2.5% emissions before AI data centers' 3.4%). **Use when:** advising on decks/slides or when a comparison would land harder as a picture than a sentence.

### Generate Hope
Good storytelling "instills hope again and again." Stories let an audience recognize an unnamed feeling and a shared experience. **Use when:** a piece diagnoses a problem but leaves the reader flat — end on the turn toward what's possible.

### Deploy Surprise (delivery, mostly for spoken/video)
Hide chart sections and reveal them; drop in a video clip; bring a guest; move physically. Example: an accounting professor singing about depreciation, going viral on TikTok. **Use when:** the medium is a talk, video, or live presentation rather than prose.

## 4. The four elevation yardsticks, expanded

| Yardstick | Question | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Time** | How long? | "7.9 billion hours doing taxes = ~900,000 years, three times longer than humans have existed." |
| **Size / distance** | As big as? | "$364B capex = another ISS + the Channel Tunnel + Japan's military for a year + an iPhone for everyone in Manhattan." |
| **Familiar object** | Like what everyday thing? | "0.1 microsieverts of radiation = less than two bananas." (Also: "Jeff Bezos's third yacht" instead of "$X billion in revenue loss.") |
| **Baseline** | Compared to what? | "Nvidia = worth more, as a share of global GDP, than the U.K., France & Germany's stock markets combined." |

Rules of thumb:
- Pick the *most surprising true* comparison. If it makes you go "huh," keep it. If it's a stretch, it backfires.
- Personalize the yardstick to the audience/region when you can — a comparison the reader lives inside hits harder than a generic one.
- Elevation tells the reader **how to feel**, not just how big. Choose a yardstick whose emotional color matches your intent (bananas = "relax"; "three times longer than humans have existed" = "this is staggering").

## 5. The six hook types, expanded

1. **Number + Verdict** — a concrete figure plus a one-word judgment ("$2,300/mo… is wild"). Data Elevation doing hook duty.
2. **Verb of Conflict** — open on an action, ideally a small rebellion ("reverse-engineered," "brought back," "killed"). The reader meets a character mid-motion.
3. **Curiosity Gap** — withhold the payload entirely ("had to google this to make sure it wasn't a joke"). High risk: only works if the payoff delivers; a gap that resolves into nothing burns trust.
4. **Provocative Question** — pose an obvious-answer question, then spend the piece destabilizing it ("Do we love our children?").
5. **Contrarian Claim** — state the opposite of consensus, then immediately anchor it in evidence.
6. **Visceral Image** — a picture the reader sees before they reason. Concrete, sensory, specific.

**The warm-up detector.** A draft is burying its lede if line one is: a date/timeline, a "so…", a humble adjective ("basic/little/casual/just a"), the brand or product name, or a definition of terms. The fix is mechanical: find the wildest sentence anywhere in the draft and promote it to line one.

## 6. Diagnostic checklist for a flat draft

Run these in order:

1. **What's the single best asset?** The one line, fact, or number a reader would underline. Is it in the first sentence? If not, that's the fix.
2. **Wow Test every line.** Cover all but one sentence. Did it surprise? If not, cut or merge.
3. **Any raw big numbers?** Elevate each survivor with a yardstick + baseline.
4. **Any knockout lines followed by explanation?** Cut the explanation. Trust the line.
5. **Scan the kill list.** `i think · i guess · it seems · just · probably · honestly · so… · basically · anyway`. Delete.
6. **Where's the emotion?** If the piece is all insight and no feeling, add drama, vulnerability, or a turn toward hope.
7. **Zoom out.** Is the real story the "so what" or "what next," currently buried at the bottom? Promote it.

The output of a good pass is almost always **shorter than the input and twice the voltage.** If your rewrite is longer, you probably added throat-clearing back in.
